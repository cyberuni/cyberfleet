A **ship** is a project. Its home is the project's main checkout, and it opens as one herdr
workspace with three tabs. The **Command Center** is a second kind of workspace with two tabs: it
holds the one Operator and the fleet-level roles. Both open on a **Dashboard**. The scenarios in this
section all happen on these layouts:

- [Work from a ship's remote pane](/cyberfleet/scenarios/work-from-the-dashboard/): the Council asks the one Operator for work from inside a ship.
- [Work from the Command Center](/cyberfleet/scenarios/work-from-the-command-center/): the Council asks the Operator directly, and the Operator relays to the ship's Captain.
- [Landing a pull request](/cyberfleet/scenarios/landing-a-pull-request/): a Pod's pull request reaches trunk through the Coordinator.
- [Work across ships](/cyberfleet/scenarios/work-across-ships/): a Captain needs work from another ship.
- [Captains in dispute](/cyberfleet/scenarios/captains-in-dispute/): two Captains disagree, and the dispute needs settling.

## The crew

| Automaton | Seat | Model tier | Starts | Job |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Council** | Wherever you are | Human | — | Gives directions and makes decisions. All authority starts here. |
| **Operator** | Command Center Dashboard, left pane. There is one. | Mid (Sonnet) | With the Command Center | Your voice to the fleet. Answers status itself and relays work and orders to the target ship. |
| **Captain** | A ship's Bridge, left pane. One per ship, plus one for the fleet on the Flag Bridge. | High (Opus) | On demand | Decides. Judges a request's freshness, priority, and scope, owns the issue tracker and the plan, spawns and manages Pods, reviews what landed, and rules on escalations. The fleet's Captain makes the roadmap and cross-ship decisions. |
| **Coordinator** | A ship's Bridge, right pane. One per ship, plus one for the fleet on the Flag Bridge. | Low (Haiku) | On demand | A ship's lands pull requests: it applies the clean bar, orders rebases, merges clean work, and escalates judgment. The fleet's does the mechanics for the fleet's Captain, for issues in the fleet's tracker. |
| **Pod** | Its own herdr workspace and git worktree | Set per Pod by the Captain | Spawned by the Captain, or by a Pod for a gate | Carries one line of work (implementation, judge, refactor, audit) in one mission. |
| **Mediator** | Cold | High | Per dispute | Writes a brief of a dispute. Decides nothing. |

A ship has no Operator. The Dashboard's left pane is the **remote pane**, a mid-tier session for
talking to the one Operator and directing traffic from inside the ship. The fleet's Captain is
admiral-like, and is still called a Captain.

The tiers follow how often each role acts and how much each action matters. The Captain takes few
turns, and each one changes the plan. The Coordinator takes many turns, and most are a check and a
command. The Operator holds a conversation. Starting the Captain and the Coordinator on demand means
the Opus session costs nothing while there is nothing to decide.

## Where the Council talks

Each pane is a place to talk on purpose. The remote pane and the Operator's seat are the mid tier.
Talking in a Captain's pane is a deliberate choice to spend the high tier. The Coordinator's pane is
where you speak to the landing directly.

You start a Captain by invoking `/captain` in that Captain's pane. The session becomes the Captain
and takes its lease. Anywhere else, `/operator` and `/captain` **call** the existing automaton and
never make the calling session one: the session passes your words, verbatim, to the role by
mentioning its handle, and shows the reply.

Orders travel as they do today (authority-governance §3). Wherever you give an order, the automaton
that receives it relays your words, verbatim and as a turn, to the unit that acts, unless it is that
unit.

Every layout is a template slot you can change.

### Dashboard (ship)

The first tab, running in the main checkout. The default puts the remote pane on the left, where you
talk to the Operator and direct traffic, and a status UI on the right that shows the ship's issues,
pull requests, and status. The UI is any program that renders that state, such as a refreshing
`cyberfleet status` view until the mission TUI replaces it.

### Bridge

The second tab. The Captain's session is on the left, and the Coordinator's session is on the right.
Both panes stay empty until something needs them. You start the Captain with `/captain` in its pane.
When a post that needs an answer mentions a Captain or a Coordinator that is not running, the
mention starts it through its lease, so a ship you only ask questions of never starts an Opus session.

### Engine Room

The third tab, for long-running processes the work needs: Storybook, the docs site, a dev server.
These are processes for the main checkout, not agents. A Pod that needs a preview of its own branch
starts one in its own worktree.

### Command Center

The Dashboard holds the Operator's seat on the left and fleet status on the right. The Flag Bridge
holds the fleet's Captain on the left, where the fleet's roadmap and cross-ship decisions are made,
and the fleet's Coordinator on the right. Both start on demand.

## Missions and their channels

The fleet's roles sit on the tracker's levels, read from the tracker and never inferred.

| Tracker level | Fleet role | Channel |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Issue, with any sub-issues | **Mission**: an issue in flight. One Captain, one lease. It may land several pull requests and ends when the Captain closes the issue. It may have **sub-missions**, and may be blocked by a mission in another ship. | The **mission channel** |
| Subtask: not releasable on its own | A **line of work** in a mission, carried by Pods | A thread on the mission channel, holding its SDD-mission's ledger |

Everything is a mission. A Captain adds sub-missions to break work down. Crossing a repository
boundary makes the work a dependency: a mission in the other repository that blocks this one, because
each repository releases on its own.

The automatons talk over [cynapse](https://github.com/cyberuni/cynapse) channels, like channels in
Slack or Discord. Missions, a ship's queue, and threads have channels; roles do not. A role is
reached by mentioning its **handle**: `cyberlegion/captain`, `cyberlegion/coordinator`, `operator`,
`fleet/captain`, `cyberlegion/pod-7a`. The ship's part of a handle is the repository name. An
exchange that needs different readers from its parent is a **thread**: a child channel anchored at
the entry that starts it. A line of work, a gate (whose cold judge reads only its thread), an
escalation with its ruling, a dispute, and an item in the ship queue are threads. Every thread posts
its outcome back to its parent, so a reader of the parent never has to open it.

| Channel | Members | Carries |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Mission, one per issue | The Operator that brought it and the ship's Captain; each Pod joins its own mission's | The Council's words as the first entry, the plan, decisions and rulings, the delegation record, and the outcome. For a mission with sub-missions or blockers, only what crosses them: the plan, blockers, invitations, and the delegation record |
| Line-of-work thread, on the mission channel | The Pods carrying the line | The Pod's progress and done reports, and its SDD-mission's ledger |
| Gate, escalation, or dispute thread | The parties to it | One exchange, and its outcome posted back to its parent |
| Ship queue, one per ship | The Captain and the Coordinator; a Pod joins when it posts | One thread per item: "PR ready" from Pods, tracker mechanics from the Captain, and landing talk. Two pull requests that conflict in meaning are escalated here |

The Coordinator joins no mission channel, and the ship's remote pane is never invited to one. No
pull request channel is opened. Review lives on the host, and landing talk lives in the pull request's
queue thread. A starting Captain rereads the channels of its open missions and its ship queue.

A **mention** is a ping. It wakes every live session acting as the handle. When none is running and
the post needs an answer (a request, an escalation, a decision-request), it starts one through the
handle's lease. A notification, such as an outcome, only wakes. Waking and starting are the
runtime's job.

## Features this layout needs

| Need | Provided by | Status |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A per-role model and effort | `cyberlegion spawn --model --effort`, also accepted by `service start` | Built |
| Start a role on demand, at most once, with a fenced lease | `cyberlegion service start <project> <name>` | Built |
| Mission and queue channels with members, and anchored threads | `cynapse channel create --kind work --member --anchor` | Built in cynapse; cyberfleet does not depend on cynapse yet. How the queue is keyed apart from cynapse's repository address channel is open |
| Own the ships: name, remote, template, tracker routing, landing setting, and the path to each main checkout | `cyberfleet ship` commands | Gap: replaces the project address; the runtime is handed paths and handles |
| Open a ship or the Command Center as a workspace with named tabs and a pane layout | cyber-mux layout templates | cyber-mux has templates; cyberlegion does not call them, and no `cyberfleet` verb opens a ship |
| Start a session in a named slot, such as the Bridge's right pane | `cyberlegion spawn --at` | Gap: `--at` only places relative to the caller (`pane:right`, `pane:down`, `tab`, `workspace`), and tabs are unnamed |
| A mention that wakes every live session acting as a handle, and starts one through the handle's lease only when the post needs an answer | The runtime's wake and lease | Gap: the runtime must accept a handle as an opaque lease key (ADR-0030) |
| `/captain` and `/operator` as a call from any pane | Operator and Captain personas | Gap: neither persona is rewritten for it yet |
