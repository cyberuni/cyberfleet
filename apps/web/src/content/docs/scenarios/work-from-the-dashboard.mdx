You are in a ship's Dashboard tab, in the **remote pane**: a mid-tier session for talking to the
one Operator and directing traffic. The ship has no Operator of its own. You ask for a change: "Add
retry with backoff to the uploader." This page follows that request until the work is on trunk.

## Who is involved

| Automaton | Tier | Role in this scenario |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Council | Human | Asks for the work, and later answers the merge announcement |
| Remote pane | Sonnet | Calls the Operator with your words and shows the reply |
| Operator | Sonnet | Takes the request, files or finds the issue, opens the mission channel, and reports back |
| Captain | Opus | Takes the issue in, judges it, writes the plan, spawns Pods, and later reviews the settled queue |
| Pod | Set by the Captain | Carries one line of work in a worktree and opens the pull request |
| Coordinator | Haiku | Lands the pull requests. See [Landing a pull request](/cyberfleet/scenarios/landing-a-pull-request/) |

## Step by step

### 1. The Council asks, and the remote pane calls

Your message is a turn in the remote pane's session, so it is an order
([authority-governance](https://github.com/cyberuni/cyberfleet/blob/main/packages/cyberfleet/skills/authority-governance/SKILL.md) §1).
The remote pane acts for the Operator by **calling** it: it passes your words, verbatim, to the
Operator by mentioning its handle, `operator`, and shows the reply. Calling takes over nothing, and
the remote pane never becomes the Operator. The Operator first decides what kind of turn it received:

- **A question** ("what's open?", "is PR 57 green?") the Operator answers itself, from the status
  UI's sources: `cyberfleet status`, `gh`, and channels. No Captain is woken.
- **Work**, as here, goes up to the ship's Captain. The Operator does not judge priority or scope
  itself.

Because this order dispatches work, the Operator also announces that the ship's Coordinator lands
the resulting pull requests, and names the clean bar. Your reply delegates the merge to that
Coordinator, for this request's pull requests. The reply reaches the Operator through the call, and
the Operator relays it onward in step 6.

### 2. The Operator opens the mission

The Operator files the issue, or finds the one you named, and resolves its ship from the ship
registry by the issue's normalized remote. If the repository is not cloned on this machine, that is
your call: the Operator asks, suggesting a path, then clones and registers the ship. It then opens
the mission channel, keyed by the issue, posts your words as its first entry without paraphrasing
them, and mentions the Captain. The members are the Operator and the ship's Captain. The remote pane
is not invited.

```sh
cyberfleet ship show <ship>
cynapse channel create <handle> --kind work --type gh.issue --title "<title>" \
  --store gh --native-id <issue-node-id> --member operator --member <ship>/captain
cynapse entry append <mission-channel> --body "<the Council's words>"
```

The mention is a request, which needs an answer, so it starts the Captain through its lease if no
session is acting as `<ship>/captain`. Two mentions that race start one session. The Operator then
waits for the reply on the mission channel with `cynapse entry wait <entry>`.

### 3. The Captain judges the request

The Captain reads the mission channel it was mentioned on and judges the request on three points:

- **Freshness.** Is it already tracked, already done, or stale? It searches the tracker
  (`gh issue list --search`) and reads the channels of its open missions and its ship queue.
- **Priority.** Where does it sit against the open plan? This is where the Opus tier earns its cost.
- **Details.** Scope, acceptance, and how to split it into lines of work.

It then takes the issue in, updating it if its scope needs to change (`gh issue edit`). The issue is
now a **mission** in flight, which may land several pull requests and ends when the Captain closes
the issue. The plan lives in the tracker. If the work turns out to span repositories, the Captain
files a mission in the other repository that blocks this one; that case is
[Work across ships](/cyberfleet/scenarios/work-across-ships/).

### 4. The Captain spawns Pods

The Captain plans the lines of work and any blocking links between them, then calls the frontier
command for the work whose blockers are all closed. For each line of work it starts, it spawns a Pod
into its own worktree and chooses the Pod's model:

```sh
cyberlegion spawn --agent pod --model sonnet --branch <branch> --base main \
  --brief-file <brief> --at workspace
```

Then the Captain records its handle as the Pod's owner, at the lease generation it holds, and opens
the line of work's thread on the mission channel:

```sh
cyberfleet pod bind <pod> --generation <n> --mission <issue-ref>
```

The owner is the Captain's handle, not this session, so the next Captain session has the Pod without
adopting it.

The brief names the line of work's thread, so the Pod reports there, and names the ship queue, so it
knows where to say its pull request is ready. The thread holds the line's SDD-mission ledger. A Pod
that needs a gate opens the gate thread and spawns the judge itself. The judge has no Pod record of
its own, but like everything in the ship it is the Captain's: the Captain finds it through its gate
thread, and closes it if its Pod is gone. The Captain never directs its verdict. The Captain manages
Pods and never works inside one.

### 5. The Captain replies, and the Operator reports

The Captain posts the issue, its priority, and the Pods it spawned on the mission channel, and
mentions the Operator. That is a notification, so it wakes a running Operator and starts none. The
Operator's `entry wait` returns, and it tells you through the remote pane in a sentence. After that,
the Captain has nothing to do until something needs a decision or the Coordinator reports, so its
session can idle or exit.

### 6. The Operator relays the delegation

The Operator sends your merge words to the ship's Coordinator, verbatim, as a turn, starting the
Coordinator through its lease if it is not running. The quote, its scope, and who relayed it go on
the mission channel as the audit record. The record is not the delegation: a Coordinator that reads
it is reading fetched content (authority-governance §3). The delegation is the turn in the
Coordinator's own session, and it covers this request's pull requests. The Coordinator joins no
mission channel.

```sh
cyberlegion send --start <ship>/coordinator --message "<the Council's words>"
cynapse entry append <mission-channel> --type delegation.record --body "<quote, scope, relayed by>"
```

### 7. Pods work, and hand off to the Coordinator

Each Pod works in its worktree and opens a pull request. When its line of work is done, it reports
in its thread and posts "PR ready" as a thread in the ship queue, which it joins by posting:

```sh
cynapse entry append <line-of-work-thread> --type pod.report --ref gh:<org>/<repo>#57
cynapse entry append <ship-queue> --type pr.ready --ref gh:<org>/<repo>#57
```

The Coordinator counts open pull requests: up when a Pod opens one, down when it merges or closes.
From here the work is landed as described in
[Landing a pull request](/cyberfleet/scenarios/landing-a-pull-request/).

## What changes from ADR-0023

- **There is one Operator.** ADR-0023's Operator was reached from any session. Here it sits in the
  Command Center's Dashboard, and a ship's remote pane and every other session reach it by calling,
  never by becoming it. Reaching it from the Command Center is
  [the next scenario](/cyberfleet/scenarios/work-from-the-command-center/).
- **The Captain no longer lands work.** ADR-0023 gives the Captain trunk, merge order, and
  retirement. Here the Coordinator lands, and the Captain keeps the plan, the tracker, and the
  decisions.
- **Model tiers are part of the design.** Each role names its tier, and `service start` passes it
  through.
