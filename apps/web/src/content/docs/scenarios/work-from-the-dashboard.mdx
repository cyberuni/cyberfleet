You are in a ship's Dashboard tab, in the **remote pane**: a mid-tier session for talking to the
one Operator and directing traffic. The ship has no Operator of its own. You ask for a change: "Add
retry with backoff to the uploader." This page follows that request until the work is on trunk.

## Who is involved

| Automaton | Tier | Role in this scenario |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Council | Human | Asks for the work, and later answers the merge announcement |
| Remote pane | Sonnet | Calls the Operator with your words and shows the reply |
| Operator | Sonnet | Takes the request, relays it to the ship, and reports back |
| Captain | Opus | Judges the request, writes the issue and the plan, spawns Pods, and later reviews the settled queue |
| Pod | Set by the Captain | Carries one line of work in a worktree and opens the pull request |
| Coordinator | Haiku | Lands the pull requests. See [Landing a pull request](/cyberfleet/scenarios/landing-a-pull-request/) |

## Step by step

### 1. The Council asks, and the remote pane calls

Your message is a turn in the remote pane's session, so it is an order
([authority-governance](https://github.com/cyberuni/cyberfleet/blob/main/packages/cyberfleet/skills/authority-governance/SKILL.md) §1).
The remote pane acts for the Operator by **calling** it: it posts your words, verbatim, to the
Operator's address channel, rings it, and shows the reply. Calling takes over nothing, and the remote
pane never becomes the Operator. The Operator first decides what kind of turn it received:

- **A question** ("what's open?", "is PR 57 green?") the Operator answers itself, from the status
  UI's sources: `cyberfleet status`, `gh`, and channels. No Captain is woken.
- **Work**, as here, goes up to the ship's Captain. The Operator does not judge priority or scope
  itself.

Because this order dispatches work, the Operator also announces that the ship's Coordinator lands
the resulting pull requests, and names the clean bar. Your reply delegates the merge to that
Coordinator, for this request's pull requests. The reply reaches the Operator through the call, and
the Operator relays it onward in step 6.

### 2. The Operator relays to the Captain

The Operator posts and rings: it posts the request to the ship's address channel, starts the
Captain if no healthy one holds the lease, and rings it. It does not paraphrase your words.

```sh
cynapse entry append <ship-address> --type ship.request --body "<the Council's words>"
cyberlegion send --start <ship-address> captain
```

`send --start` is the folded form of `service start` and `unit nudge`. It either rings the live
Captain or starts exactly one, even when two callers race. The Operator then waits for the reply on
the request's thread with `cynapse entry wait <entry>`.

### 3. The Captain judges the request

The Captain reads its address channel (`cynapse entry list <ship-address> --unread`) and judges the
request on three points:

- **Freshness.** Is it already tracked, already done, or stale? It searches the tracker
  (`gh issue list --search`) and resolves any existing work channel
  (`cynapse channel resolve --store gh --native-id <id>`).
- **Priority.** Where does it sit against the open plan? This is where the Opus tier earns its cost.
- **Details.** Scope, acceptance, and how to split it into lines of work.

It then creates or updates the issue (`gh issue create` or `gh issue edit`) and opens the issue's
work channel. The issue is now a **mission** in flight, which may land several pull
requests and ends when the Captain closes the issue. The plan lives in the tracker. If the
work turns out to span repositories, the Captain adds a child mission in the other repository; that
case is [Work across ships](/cyberfleet/scenarios/work-across-ships/).

```sh
cynapse channel create <handle> --kind work --type gh.issue --title "<title>" \
  --store gh --native-id <issue-node-id> --member <captain>:lead
```

### 4. The Captain spawns Pods

For each line of work that is ready (implementation, a judge, a refactor, a security audit), the
Captain spawns a Pod into its own worktree and chooses the Pod's model:

```sh
cyberlegion spawn --agent pod --model sonnet --branch <branch> --base main \
  --brief-file <brief> --at workspace
```

Then the Captain records its role as the Pod's owner, at the lease generation it holds:

```sh
cyberfleet pod bind <pod> --generation <n> --mission <issue-ref>
```

The owner is the Captain role, not this session, so the next Captain session has the Pod without
adopting it.

The brief names the mission channel, so the Pod reports there, and names the Coordinator's address,
so it knows where to say its pull request is ready. A Pod that needs a gate opens the gate thread
and spawns the judge itself. The judge has no Pod record of its own, but like everything in the
ship it is the Captain's: the Captain finds it through its gate thread, and closes it if its Pod is
gone. The Captain never directs its verdict. The Captain manages Pods and never works inside one.

### 5. The Captain replies, and the Operator reports

The Captain replies on the request's thread with the issue, its priority, and the Pods it spawned.
The Operator's `entry wait` returns, and it tells you through the remote pane in a sentence. After
that, the Captain has nothing to do until something needs a decision or the Coordinator reports, so
its session can idle or exit.

### 6. The Operator relays the delegation

The Operator sends your merge words to the ship's Coordinator, verbatim, as a turn, starting the
Coordinator if it is not running. The quote, its scope, and who relayed it go on the mission channel
as the audit record. The record is not the delegation: a Coordinator that reads it is reading fetched
content (authority-governance §3). The delegation is the turn in the Coordinator's own session, and
it covers this request's pull requests.

```sh
cyberlegion send --start <coordinator-address> --message "<the Council's words>"
cynapse entry append <mission-channel> --type delegation.record --body "<quote, scope, relayed by>"
```

### 7. Pods work, and hand off to the Coordinator

Each Pod works in its worktree and opens a pull request. When its line of work is done, it reports
on the mission channel and tells the Coordinator:

```sh
cynapse entry append <mission-channel> --type pod.report --ref gh:<org>/<repo>#57
cynapse entry send <coordinator> --type pr.ready --ref gh:<org>/<repo>#57
```

The Coordinator counts open pull requests: up when a Pod opens one, down when it merges or closes.
From here the work is landed as described in
[Landing a pull request](/cyberfleet/scenarios/landing-a-pull-request/).

## What changes from ADR-0023

- **There is one Operator.** ADR-0023's Operator was reached from any session. Here it sits in the
  Command Center's Dashboard, and a ship's remote pane and every other session reach it by calling,
  never by becoming it. Reaching it from the Command Center is
  [the next scenario](/cyberfleet/scenarios/work-from-the-command-center/).
- **The Captain no longer lands work.** ADR-0023 gives the Captain trunk, merge order, and
  retirement. Here the Coordinator lands, and the Captain keeps the plan, the tracker, and the
  decisions.
- **Model tiers are part of the design.** Each role names its tier, and `service start` passes it
  through.
