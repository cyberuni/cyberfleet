Continuing [Work across ships](/cyberfleet/scenarios/work-across-ships/): Captain B declines A's
request ("the extension point already exists; use it in A"), and Captain A disagrees ("the extension
point cannot express this; the fix belongs in B"). Other disputes take the same path:

- both Captains claim the same fix, or both decline it;
- two ships each need to land a breaking change first;
- two Captains read one change and derive conflicting intents from it.

There is no lead Captain, and a Captain has no authority in another ship
(authority-governance §2). So no Captain can settle a dispute by ruling. The process below settles
most disputes without the Council, and brings the rest to the Council already summarized.

## Who is involved

| Automaton | Tier | Role in this scenario |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Captain A and Captain B | Opus | State their positions, and apply standing rules |
| Mediator | Opus, headless, cold | Writes a brief of both positions with a recommendation. Decides nothing. |
| Command Center Operator | Sonnet | Starts the mediator, puts the brief to the Council, and relays the answer |
| Council | — | Decides any dispute the rules do not settle |

## Step by step

### 1. Open an arbitration channel

The Captain that hits the disagreement branches an arbitration channel from the disputed entry, with
both Captains as members. Arguing moves out of the work channel, so the Pods' reports stay readable.

```sh
cynapse channel create <handle> --kind work --type fleet.arbitration --title "<the question>" \
  --anchor <disputed-entry> --member <captain-a>:party --member <captain-b>:party
cyberlegion unit nudge <other-captain-handle>
```

Meanwhile both Captains hold the disputed sorties and keep working everything else. Nobody acts on
the disputed point while it is open.

### 2. State positions, with a bound

Each Captain posts one position: the claim, the evidence, the cost to its ship, and what would change
its mind. Each gets one rebuttal. The bound matters, because two Opus sessions arguing without one is
the most expensive loop the fleet can run. If they agree, either Captain records the outcome and the
channel closes.

### 3. Apply standing rules

Many disputes repeat, so the Council writes rules for them in advance, and either Captain can settle
a dispute by citing one. For example:

- **The owner of an artifact decides about that artifact.** B decides B's API; A decides how A uses
  it.
- **A ratified decision outranks an unratified one.**
- **A green trunk and a released contract outrank convenience.** The change that keeps both is
  preferred.

The Captains apply the rules; they never write or widen them. Writing one is widening a delegation,
which is ratification-class (authority-governance §5).

### 4. Mediate, then put it to the Council

If no rule settles it, either Captain asks the Command Center Operator for mediation. The Operator
starts a **mediator**: a fresh, headless Opus session that has worked for neither ship. It reads the
arbitration channel and both issues, and writes a brief: each position in its own words, the
options, what each option costs each ship, and a recommendation.

```sh
cyberlegion spawn --agent mediator --model opus --cwd <home> --brief-file <arbitration-brief>
```

The mediator decides nothing. A decision binding two ships is cross-project authority, which
reaches ships only from the Council through the Command Center.

### 5. The Council decides, and the Operator relays

The Operator puts the brief to you. Your answer goes to each Captain, as you wrote it, as a turn
(`cyberlegion unit nudge --message`), and onto the arbitration channel as the record. Each Captain
applies it within its own ship. If the decision becomes a pattern, it is a candidate for a new
standing rule, and only you can add one.

## Why a mediator, not one of the Captains

Either Captain summarizing the dispute would present its own side best. A cold session reads both
sides from the channel with no stake and no context from either ship. The Command Center Operator
could write the brief itself, but it runs on the mid tier, and weighing two plans against each other
is the judgment the high tier is for.
