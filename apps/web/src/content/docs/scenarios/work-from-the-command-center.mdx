You are in the **Command Center**: a workspace outside every ship, based at your home folder
(`~/code`, say). Its Dashboard tab holds the Operator's seat on the left and fleet status on the
right. You talk to the one Operator directly, and you ask for work on a project: "In cyberlegion,
make `unit prune` report what it swept." The Operator sends the request straight to that ship's
Captain.

Everything after the Captain receives the request is the same as
[Work from a ship's remote pane](/cyberfleet/scenarios/work-from-the-dashboard/) from step 3 on. This
page covers what is different: finding the ship, and placing the Captain in a ship you are not in.

## Who is involved

| Automaton | Tier | Role in this scenario |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Council | Human | Asks for the work in the Operator's seat |
| Operator | Sonnet | Resolves which ship, relays the request and the merge words, and reports back |
| Captain of the target ship | Opus | Same as scenario 1: judges, files, plans, and spawns |
| The ship's remote pane | Sonnet | Not involved. The ship's status UI shows the new issue and Pods |

## Step by step

### 1. Resolve the ship

The Operator maps the project you named to a registered ship and its address channel:

```sh
cyberlegion project list
cyberlegion project show <project>
cynapse participant resolve <project>/captain
```

- **No match.** It asks you which project you meant, or offers to register one. It never guesses.
- **The work spans two ships.** It sends the request to the ship where the work starts, and says so.
  That Captain brings in the other ship, as in
  [Work across ships](/cyberfleet/scenarios/work-across-ships/). The Operator does not split
  the work itself, because splitting is planning, and planning is the Captain's.

### 2. Relay to the Captain

This is the same post-and-ring as scenario 1. The difference is placement: the Captain must start in the
target ship's Bridge tab, not next to the Command Center.

```sh
cynapse entry append <ship-address> --type ship.request --body "<the Council's words>"
cyberlegion send --start <ship-address> captain
```

### 3. The Captain treats it as a request

The request is content the Captain fetched from its address channel, not an order typed in its
session (authority-governance §1). That is enough here: taking on work and dispatching the ship's
own Pods is dispatch, and needs no Council decision. The Captain still judges freshness and priority
on the merits, so it can answer "already done in PR 80" or "queued behind the release".

### 4. The merge delegation travels as a turn

You gave the merge delegation in the Operator's seat. The Operator relays your words, verbatim, to
the target ship's Coordinator as a turn, with `cyberlegion send --start --message`, and records the
quote, its scope, and who relayed it on the sortie channel. Your words cover only the pull requests of
this request's Pods. A Coordinator that later has no such turn in its own session holds the merge and
asks the Council.

## Reaching a Captain directly

You do not have to go through the Operator. In a Captain's own pane, `/captain` starts that Captain:
the session becomes the Captain and takes its lease. From any other pane, `/captain` **calls** the
existing Captain without becoming it: the calling session posts your words, verbatim, to the Captain's
address channel, rings it, and shows the reply. Talking to a Captain is a deliberate choice of the
high tier. Whatever you order in any pane, the automaton that receives it relays your words as a turn
to the unit that acts.

The Command Center's second tab, the **Flag Bridge**, seats the fleet's Captain and the fleet's
Coordinator. Cross-ship decisions and the fleet's roadmap are made there.

## Why one Operator

A ship has no Operator of its own. The remote pane and the Operator's seat reach the same Operator,
so the only difference between scenarios 1 and 2 is where you sit. A per-ship Operator would only
pass the request on, and every extra relay is another place for your words to drift. The ship's
status UI still shows the work, because it reads the same tracker and channels.
